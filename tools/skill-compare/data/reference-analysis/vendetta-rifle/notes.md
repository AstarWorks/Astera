# Frame-first re-analysis — wbu2viInM3w

The actual source is **24 fps, 3,436 frames, 143.166667 s**. It is not 30 fps. All JSON frame indices are zero-based source frames. Native sheets show original PTS, but their `fN` labels count selected frames; use `sheet-manifest.json` to translate those labels. Intervals are half-open. All 24 overview sheets, all 55 native sheets, and the weapon detail sheet were visually inspected. Work was based on these frames before opening the old analyses.

Body-height distances and palette hex values are estimates. Contact geometry is frequently hidden by a moving crop, smear or flash; JSON uncertainty fields distinguish visible impact evidence from an unknowable game damage timestamp. No HUD/coin icon is available to establish exact coin resolution. A preparation spark on the caster is not a hit on the target. Camera moves are described for recreation, but exact world coordinates/FOV cannot be recovered from this 2D edit.

## What the old analyses got wrong

1. The first analysis calls Sinclair a target/black-hat secondary unit. The blond, dark-clad rifle user is the actor of the four opening attacks; the short gray-haired red-clad figure is their target. Meursault is the sunglasses/red-coat cameo at 29 s and the later courtyard actor.
2. It merged 12–20 s and 24–32 s into large invented skills. There are separate S1/S2/S3 sequences, a luminous S3 repeat, then another character’s action. The new timeline covers intro, all combat/repeats, intervening gaps, artwork and logo outro.
3. Both old notes describe a detached ghost rifle rack of 7/8/10+ guns. Upright/shouldered frames at 51.000, 56.292 and 89.000 show one held segmented implement; the red fan travels with it. No independent rack, eight summoned guns, or staggered eight-gun volley is demonstrated.
4. The flipbook labels the hall cameo S2 by counting its preparation flash as a hit. It has two target-directed impacts and repeats courtyard S1. The 50.750–55.000 three-swing sequence fits S2 better than the flipbook’s S3-1 assignment.
5. Yellow/green opponent attacks and clash rings were labelled Meursault Defense, dodge or parry. Ownership and actual counter candidate are now separated.
6. The long late sequences were labelled E.G.O-like and combined into a single 30-second finisher. They are two distinct repeated expanded weapon sequences after a state change, best matched to S3-2; no E.G.O identifier is present.
7. Old notes called orange flares automatically Tremor Burst, red jagged VFX physical blood crystals, and straight trails laser gunfire. Color/shape alone cannot establish those mechanics or physical props. Many red eruptions happen at the caster during charge while the target is elsewhere. Straight streaks accompany body movement; no detached bullets or sustained stationary beam attack is established.
8. Old block distances, 20-tick beat recipes, exact particle counts, six-round capacity, ammo-on-melee generation, Bloodlust stacks, Bleed detonations and parry refunds were invented or contradicted the supplied kit. None is carried forward.
9. Coin counts are not the number of every flash/crescent. The supplied kit and observed 4/7-strike longer animations cannot be made one-to-one with certainty. The JSON preserves all visible beats and leaves unverified coin assignments null.

## Files

`segments.json` is the continuous full-video partition; character-prefixed segment files are filtered indices. Each character has separate skill files, with repeats embedded as timed instances. `sinclair-extra-1.json` indexes the luminous S3 repeat. `meursault-extra-1.json` contains S3-2 and its gold-aura repeat; `extra-2` contains state/reload animation; `extra-3` records opponent attacks. `sheet-manifest.json` maps every kept contact sheet. `build_analysis.py` reproduces the JSON from the manually reviewed observations; it does not infer hits from audio or image brightness.
